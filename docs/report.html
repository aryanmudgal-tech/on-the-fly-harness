Research verdict · agent harnesses · revised 9 September 2026

Reimagining the Agent Harness

Nineteen research documents, each fact-checked by a second agent, tested one thesis: as models get better, the harness becomes the bottleneck, and neither a CLI nor a desktop app can be the default way people operate agents. This page is the verdict, the design that follows from it, and the startup recommendation. The full corpus, with every source and every claim's verification status, is in the repository.

Written for a software engineer new to agents · market sizing deliberately deferred · numbers carry their labels: vendor means the company that benefits reported it

The answer in brief

The thesis survives as a design thesis and fails as a broad business thesis. A harness has two layers that move in opposite directions. The inner layer (the loop, prompts, scaffolds) is small in lines, large in leverage, and belongs to whoever owns the model. The outer layer (identity, permissions, verification, memory, cost, surfaces) is where every incident and every 2026 vendor investment lives. That layer is the bottleneck.

"Not a CLI" is already the industry's own position, for the surface. Every lab runs one CLI-shaped engine behind many thin surfaces. The CLI became the engine, not the front door. Chat surfaces carry two orders of magnitude more people; developer surfaces lead on money and delegated autonomy. The desktop app is not dead: it is where the two largest labs put the window you approve things in.

For non-technical people, the part to reimagine is the trust model, not the body style. Standalone new surfaces have the worst record in the corpus. Observed demand is thin, attended and document-shaped. Authority and reliability both bind; what nobody provides is a way to check the work without expertise. The neutral control plane is not open either: its pieces already ship, and the company that owns identity and the office suite already governs rival agents.

Two candidate businesses were proposed here and both were falsified. A startup-owned authority layer died to the red team: its pieces already ship, its seat belongs to the identity provider, and it cannot enforce anything on a hosted engine. An independent verifier died to a market test prompted by one question from the repository owner. Checking code is CodeRabbit's product, at more than 17,000 customers and a $1.5B valuation as of August 2026, and it ships five of the six outputs the recommendation named. Checking claims against sources in documents and spreadsheets shipped in December 2025 and July 2026.

What is left is one hypothesis and one measurement. Nobody, in any domain, judges the agent's execution trace: what it actually did, as opposed to what it produced. Whether that is a company or a feature is decided by a single test on real agent-written changes that a reviewer already passed. The fallback is the vertical operator-configured harness, the only archetype in this corpus with proven monetization. The research produced a good map, a clear design principle, and no validated company. That is the honest result.

The two layers

Eleven findings

  1. The harness is two layers that move in opposite directions, and the inner one belongs to the model's owner. Small in lines, large in leverage: use the vendor's default, re-simplify on upgrades, never make it a moat.
  2. "One runtime, many surfaces" is the settled shape. Anthropic's docs: each surface connects to the same engine. Codex is one Rust core under a CLI, an IDE, a desktop app and a cloud. Open harnesses reached hundreds of thousands of stars in weeks.
  3. Standalone new surfaces have the worst record; embedded surfaces have the revenue. Operator, Atlas, Project Mariner, Humane, Dia, Manus and Poke were folded, sold, stalled or shut within a year; Agentforce, ServiceNow, Glean and Copilot live inside tools people already use.
  4. Non-technical delegation is thin, attended and document-shaped; authority and reliability both bind. Seventy-six percent of Claude conversations match non-computing tasks, mostly search, reference and editing; the best model passes half of Zapier's business tasks; "a strategy memo doesn't compile."
  5. Approvals are divergent across vendors, and policy is centralizing in the identity provider. Dust's tool stakes, MCP's risk annotations, Scout's sign-off, Cowork's spend caps exist; what does not travel is per-person policy across vendors, and the identity provider is its natural owner.
  6. Security failures are structural and cluster where the agent reads untrusted content. The defenses that hold are an OS boundary, a credential-holding proxy, a second model, and breaking the trifecta per task. A component that never acts carries no blast radius.
  7. The labs hold the meter and rewrite the terms. Cursor's gross margin was negative until it shipped its own models; Anthropic's third-party terms changed five times in eight months, ending with the largest third-party harness cut off again in September.
  8. Protocols separated surface from runtime; the labs own the tool and agent protocols, not the interface ones. Hooks with near-identical event names in every harness are the cheapest place for an outsider to observe every action.
  9. Automated harness design is real, overfits, and the layer that matters has no benchmark of the operator's own work. Learned harnesses transfer about 0.6 points in the best independent test; business-task benchmarks exist, but nothing measures the operator's own task on the operator's own data.
  10. Generated interfaces are an output surface; the spreadsheet is the verification surface that already works. The office suite went agentic in April and May 2026; the grid is where a non-expert can check work cell by cell.
  11. Background execution is the real surface shift; interruption policy is thin and its evidence is anecdotal. The loop left the foreground; one withdrawn product with an unstated cause is not a natural experiment.

Every authority component has an owner already (directory identities, tool stakes, spend caps, classifier-gated approvals). What an outsider can own is the normalized vocabulary, the independent proof, provenance rendered into the office suite, and the corpus of verified and unverified artifacts that no evaluation covers today.

Recommendation, after two falsifications

Do not build yet. The two markets the earlier recommendation proposed are occupied. Checking code is CodeRabbit's product: it reads the linked ticket, executes checks in a sandbox, drops findings it cannot ground, blocks merges through required status checks, and markets itself with the word "independent". Checking claims against sources in documents shipped as a Word add-in in December 2025; the same idea for spreadsheet numbers shipped in July 2026, sold on the argument that the agent which wrote an answer is the worst-placed agent to grade it. That was this program's own argument, already in market.

One narrow hypothesis survives. No product found, in any domain, judges the producing agent's execution trace. The negative is primary-grade on two of them: CodeRabbit's own harness skill sends code diffs and names no other input, and Claude Code's reviewer analyses the diff and surrounding code. A trace covers a defect class a diff cannot show, such as a deleted failing test, a disabled lint rule, or a live endpoint called mid-run that never reaches the repository. It is also what an auditor asks for: a February 2026 control framework requires an audit trail of prompts, inputs, outputs, model versions and evidence of human review.

Why it may still be a feature

  • The platform owner already captures the trace and renders it beside the diff, and owns the merge gate.
  • A trace is a self-report; anything load-bearing must be re-derived against the repository, which the incumbent already does by executing code.
  • The incumbent's reviewer already runs inside agent sessions, where the transcript is a file on disk.
  • An evidence-layer product already captures local agent sessions across four harnesses.

The one test that settles it

  • Take 500 agent-written changes a leading reviewer already passed clean.
  • Recover each session trace.
  • Measure how many carry a defect visible only in the trace.
  • Measure how many of those a sandbox would have caught by re-executing the repository. This is the decisive number: if re-execution finds them, the product is a better sandbox and the incumbent owns it.

Run the measurement before any interviews. Interviews will restate what the corpus already says. If the test fails, the fallback is the vertical operator-configured harness, where a non-technical operator edits policy and the vendor owns the loop: the only archetype in this research with reliable monetization.

How the recommendation was falsified, twice

First, an adversarial reviewer agent was asked to find where the synthesis is wrong. Its critique and the response are at docs/08-synthesis/04-red-team.md. Then the repository owner asked one question, "isn't this what CodeRabbit does?", and a nine-agent market test answered it: yes. That record is at docs/08-synthesis/05-market-test.md.

Its verdict: the corpus was better than the synthesis built on it. It showed that the first recommendation reported the friendlier of two accounts of Anthropic's third-party policy, called existing designs new, proposed a control layer that cannot enforce on hosted engines and holds the blast radius, ignored gross margin, and inverted the strategy document's own ranking. Its alternative, an independent verifier, was better supported by the same evidence, and the coordinator adopted it. Three items were pushed back on at the time, one of which the market test then settled against me: the developer market is not merely crowded, it is occupied.

Both failures share one cause worth stating plainly. The research profiled harnesses and never profiled the tools that check what harnesses produce. Nineteen documents and roughly 640 fact-checked claims could not catch a question nobody asked. A verification pass tests whether the answers are right, not whether the scope was.
