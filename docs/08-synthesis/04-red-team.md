# Red team: where the synthesis is weakest
*Research program: agent harnesses. Written 2026-09-09 by an adversarial reviewer agent. Status: critique; the coordinator's response follows in the same file.*

Scope note. I read `01-findings.md`, `02-reimagined-harness.md` and `03-recommendation.md`, then all nineteen research documents including their verification notes (which override body text where they differ). Three research documents are still marked "draft, pending verification": `docs/03-building-blocks/runtime-and-safety.md`, `docs/06-users/non-technical.md` and `docs/02-landscape/developer-harness-matrix.md`. The synthesis never says so, and it leans on the first two for its most-repeated numbers. No web searches were used; every claim below is checked against the corpus only.

## Verdict in five lines

1. The research corpus is better than the synthesis built on it: the synthesis is more confident than its sources and, in the places the recommendation depends on, quotes the weaker of two disagreeing documents without saying they disagree.
2. The findings are broadly right about the landscape (the split harness, settled surfaces, the labs owning the runtime, the incident record) and wrong in three load-bearing places: the platform-risk timeline is stale and too favourable, "authority not accuracy" is contradicted by a sibling document, and most of what the design calls new has already shipped (Dust, MCP tool annotations, Microsoft Scout and Copilot Cowork).
3. The design's outer harness is a re-description of pieces Microsoft and Dust already sell, plus two parts (a cross-tool undo ledger and a generated review page used for control) that the corpus says nobody knows how to build; its flagship walkthrough keeps all three legs of the lethal trifecta and routes the request through the one surface the corpus says cannot separate the requester from bystanders.
4. The recommendation makes "the company" out of the position the strategy document rated weakest, never mentions gross margin, and asserts that the labs move slowly in a layer where the corpus records roughly a dozen authority-layer shipments in five months, several of them already cross-vendor.
5. What survives: split the harness and never own the loop; verification for non-code work is a real, unfilled gap; a vertical is the only proven monetization. What should not survive: the model-neutral authority layer as the destination, and "generate the inner harness per task" as the mechanism.

## Claims in the findings that overreach or contradict the source documents

Ranked by how much they matter to the recommendation.

**1. The platform-risk timeline is stale, and the stale version is the friendlier one.**
Finding 7: "Anthropic stopped consumer subscriptions from covering third-party harnesses on April 4, 2026, then reinstated capped credits from June 15 [strategy][independent]." The strategy document, which is the one with a primary fetch, says the opposite about June 15. `docs/07-strategy/value-capture.md` §5, table: "15 Jun 2026 | Anthropic pauses the planned move of Agent SDK, `claude -p` and third-party usage to a separate monthly credit; such usage draws on plan limits again"; "13 Jul 2026 | API error text: 'Third-party apps now draw from your extra usage, not your plan limits.'"; "3 Sep 2026 | Detection extended to `openclaw.inbound_meta.v2` and `openclaw.diagnostics.v1`; all Anthropic models unusable for subscription users without extra-usage credit." Its verification note 3e confirms the pause from the OpenClaw provider doc, fetched: "Anthropic's June 15, 2026 support update paused the previously announced separate Agent SDK credit plan." The same document's blunt summary: terms "changed at least five times in eight months." The independent document's version (credits took effect June 15) rests on search excerpts of the May 13 announcement, and its own remaining doubts say "Anthropic's reachable documentation does not describe Agent SDK credits." The matrix even records the choice: "[labs] could not re-check the reinstatement, so [independent] is treated as authoritative here." The synthesis cites [strategy] and reports [independent]. This matters because the recommendation's falsifier ("A platform decision cuts off engine access on acceptable terms, as happened ... in April 2026") is written as a one-off past event, when the primary-sourced record shows an unresolved, escalating policy that ended, as of a week ago, with the largest third-party harness cut off again.

**2. "The binding constraint is authority, not accuracy" is one side of a disagreement the synthesis does not disclose.**
Finding 4's title: "the binding constraint is authority, not accuracy." `docs/02-landscape/non-technical-startups-enterprise.md` TL;DR: "**Reliability, not interface, is the binding constraint.** Zapier's AutomationBench: the best model passes 50.3% of business tasks"; its "Contradicts" section: "The bottleneck is reliability and security, and models still move that needle ... 'Harness is the bottleneck' holds for approvals and memory, not for whether the task gets done." The synthesis's wording comes from `docs/06-users/non-technical.md` (TL;DR: "The binding barrier is authority, not accuracy"), a draft pending verification whose evidence for that line is a 20-student study read via secondary sources and a 31-participant CMU study; that document's own "Nuance" adds "Capability still limits at the edges (52.5% completion on blind users' desktop tasks; the reliability figures in the startup document)." Two documents, opposite headlines; the synthesis picks one and builds a product on it. If the agent finishes half the business tasks it is given, the authority layer governs failures, and the value sits in the half that is model-side.

**3. A two-year-old reliability number is presented as the current state.**
Finding 9: "Sierra's airline tasks pass four times in a row only 22.5% of the time [runtime][nontech-startups]." Source: "Sierra's own tau-bench (Jun 2024) found the best model then passed 46.0% of airline tasks once and 22.5% four times in a row" (`non-technical-startups-enterprise.md` §6). The runtime document's benchmark table has the 2026 successor: "tau2 / tau3-bench ... Telecom leaders about 99% (likely avg@k, secondary)." Quoting the June 2024 figure in a September 2026 synthesis, without the date, overstates the reliability gap the design is meant to fix. The same finding says "No benchmark measures whether a non-technical person's task got done"; AutomationBench is "600 public tasks, 100 each across sales, marketing, operations, support, finance and HR." The gap is narrower than claimed: what is missing is the operator's own task on the operator's own data, not business-task benchmarks as such.

**4. The design's "What is new, honestly" table is not honest about Dust, MCP and Microsoft.**
Design part 2: "What is new: consequence tags on tools, and the promotion rule for generated tools." Design table: consequence classes are "Not the unit of policy; not the same across tools or surfaces." `non-technical-startups-enterprise.md` §4, on Dust (MIT-licensed, primary source read from its repository): "every MCP tool carries a stake, `never_ask` (read-only), `low`, `medium` or `high` ('destructive or high-impact'), which 'map to review/approval expectations'; external MCP tools default to `high`." That is consequence tags as the unit of policy, with a most-restrictive default for untrusted tools, shipped and open source. `docs/03-building-blocks/protocols.md` §3.2 records MCP tool annotations "(read-only vs destructive)" in the 2025-03-26 revision and §3.7 notes the maintainers "frame tool annotations as a 'risk vocabulary'." The table's budgets row ("Not per task across engines") ignores Copilot Cowork "billed per task in credits at $0.01 each with tenant, group and user caps" with "per-task credit prices shown to the user" (`docs/02-landscape/non-technical-labs.md`, Microsoft section) and the Agent SDK's `max_budget_usd` (`docs/01-primer/anatomy.md` §3.1). The agent-as-principal row ("No consequence ceiling") ignores Scout, where "Sensitive actions can require a human to sign off" and Purview DLP is "enforced in the moment," and OpenClaw, where approvals "can only tighten config-derived security/ask, never loosen them" (`docs/05-surfaces/messaging.md` §5). What is new is integration across vendors; the pieces are not.

**5. "Every one of these standards was created by Anthropic, OpenAI or Google" is false, and the exceptions are the ones the design needs.**
Finding 8 lists MCP, instruction files, skills, hooks, the Agent Client Protocol and "the UI protocols (MCP Apps, AG-UI, A2UI, generative UI in the Vercel AI SDK)," then says "Every one of these standards was created by Anthropic, OpenAI or Google." The protocols document's sentence is about a narrower list ("MCP, A2A, the Apps SDK, A2UI, AP2, UCP, the OpenAI/Stripe ACP, AGENTS.md and Agent Skills") and elsewhere states that ACP is Zed's with JetBrains as co-lead maintainer, AG-UI is CopilotKit's and the AI SDK is Vercel's (`protocols.md` §5, §6). Its §6 conclusion is the opposite of the finding's: "AG-UI, A2UI and the AI SDK put the agent *inside an interface you own* ... A startup building a new surface lives on the second side of that line." The synthesis turned the corpus's good news for a cross-vendor layer into bad news.

**6. "The only genuinely new approval primitive found" is a messaging-document claim generalized to the corpus.**
Finding 5: "Microsoft Teams' approve-before-sharing step is the only genuinely new approval primitive found." `messaging.md` §5 says "The one new approval primitive is Teams' 'targeted messages'" inside a section about approvals in chat. Elsewhere the corpus records AP2's "three signed mandates (Intent, Cart, Payment)" and a "trusted surface" that gives "verifiable intent, not inferred action" (`protocols.md` §9); Claude Desktop's fixed computer-use tiers with a global `Esc` whose keypress "is consumed so prompt injection can't use it to dismiss dialogs" (`docs/05-surfaces/os-level.md` §2.5); Windows' "separate agent user account" (§2.1); and Dust's stakes. The finding understates how much approval design already exists, which is the premise for calling the design new.

**7. Finding 1 calls the inner harness "trivial" while its own source records double-digit swings from inner-harness changes.**
Finding 1: "The loop is trivial and getting more so." `docs/04-research/industry-engineering.md` §5, evidence for the moat: "LangChain: 52.8% to 66.5% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with gpt-5.2-codex held constant"; Anthropic's April 23, 2026 postmortem traced a regression to "a default reasoning-effort downgrade, a caching-optimization bug [...], and an overly aggressive verbosity-limiting system prompt. The model had not changed." Those are loop configuration and prompt, the inner layer. The same document's "Contradicts": "A harness 'for everyone' that abstracts over models may be structurally worse than each lab's own." `docs/01-primer/history.md` §Era 4 adds Harness-Bench and "Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness," with "double-digit swings for the same model." Small in lines is not small in leverage. The synthesis needs "trivial" so that the design can generate the inner harness per task; the evidence says a badly configured inner harness silently costs ten points, which is an argument for a stable, tested one.

**8. The Agent Builder "corpse" is still alive, and the lesson is different.**
Finding 3 lists "OpenAI's visual Agent Builder (wound down after about eight months) [labs]" among products "Failed or absorbed within a year." `docs/02-landscape/developer-harnesses-labs.md` TL;DR: "marked for wind-down on June 3, 2026: it and the Evals product are unavailable from November 30, 2026 ... users are pointed to the code-first Agents SDK or ChatGPT 'Workspace Agents'." As of today it is running; it will have lived fourteen months; and OpenAI moved non-technical agent building into ChatGPT rather than abandoning it. (The labs document's own "Supports" paragraph uses the loose "lasted about eight months," so the source is internally inconsistent; the synthesis took the loose version.)

**9. The Economic Index is used as evidence about people when the documents say it is evidence about conversations.**
Finding 4: "People hand over clerical work whole (conversations matched to clerk and secretarial tasks run 69 to 75% automation-style)." `docs/02-landscape/adoption-evidence.md` §2: "Numbers match conversation content to O*NET job tasks; they say nothing about who the users are." `non-technical.md` open question 4: "Whether high automation shares for clerical task matches reflect non-technical users or developers doing admin work: the dataset matches content, not people." The recommendation then makes this wedge criterion 1 ("Tasks people already hand over whole ... Start with the former"). Nobody in that dataset is releasing a payment; they are drafting clerical text in a chat window. Conversation style in a consumer chat app is not evidence that a mid-sized company will grant an agent authority over its accounts payable.

**10. "Policy does not travel with the person" describes user-local settings, not policy.**
Finding 5: "Policy lives in terminal and desktop artifacts, and Claude Code on the web ignores local settings, so policy does not travel with the person [protocols]." The protocols source (§8) is about `~/.claude/settings.json`; the same row says "Managed policy hooks cannot be disabled by users." Organization policy is centralizing fast: Cowork GA shipped "role-based access controls, group spend limits, usage analytics and per-tool connector controls"; Claude Tag runs under "admin-configured access"; "Enterprise-managed auth for MCP connectors (August 24, 2026) moves connector authorization into the organization's identity provider" (`non-technical-labs.md`, Anthropic section; `messaging.md` §2). The real gap is cross-vendor, per-person policy. The synthesis's phrasing implies no policy travels, and it never notices that the identity provider is where the labs are sending the problem.

**11. Principle 8 rests on one retired product whose cause of death is unknown, and on a counter-example the synthesis omits.**
Finding 11: "The one natural experiment, ChatGPT Pulse, pushed unrequested output daily and was retired in July 2026 in favour of schedules the user sets [voice]." `docs/05-surfaces/voice-ambient.md` §7: "Neither vendor published why." Pulse was Pro-only and mobile-only (§6), so cost is as good an explanation as user rejection. The same section: "Google's Daily Brief in Gemini Live (Aug 2026) is the same idea re-packaged." One withdrawn product with an unstated reason and a live replication elsewhere is not a natural experiment; it is an anecdote, and the design's interruption policy is built on it.

**12. The deals list does not fit the pattern claimed for it.**
Finding 7: "The year's deals went to distribution and compute owners: SpaceX and Cursor, Salesforce and Fin, Cognition and Poke, SAP's stake in n8n, Atlassian and Dia." Cognition is a harness company; the strategy document's actual claim is that buyers "were companies with distribution or compute ... not other harness companies" (§4), which Cognition-Poke contradicts. Atlassian-Dia closed in October 2025, not this year. Minor on its own; it shows the pattern-first, list-second habit that produced items 1 to 11.

**Where the findings are right.** The two-layer split (Finding 1's second half), the engine-not-surface reading of the CLI (Finding 2), the incident record and the structural defenses (Finding 6), "generate the view, fix the controls" (Finding 10), and the observation that no evaluation exists for the outer harness (Finding 9) all match their sources closely and survive this review.

## Evidence the synthesis ignores or underweights

Each item is present in the research documents and absent from, or contradicted by, the three synthesis documents.

**1. Observed non-technical demand is thin, attended and document-shaped; the design builds an unattended multi-app agent.**
`non-technical-labs.md`, "Contradicts": "What non-technical people actually delegate is mostly research and writing ... The elaborate multi-app harness may be ahead of demand; the thin harness (chat plus retrieval plus a document) is where usage sits." `non-technical.md`, "Contradicts": "observed delegation is thin: search, advice, editing, documents. Workers want partnership on most tasks, and 41% of startup task targets already miss what workers want; an autonomous harness may be ahead of demand." §3.6 (WORKBank): "equal partnership" dominant in 45.2% of occupations; "workers want more agency than AI experts judge necessary." Finding 4 quotes "broad and shallow" and then the design's operations manager gets one subagent per vendor, unattended, with batch approvals. Both user documents warn against exactly that product; neither warning appears in the design or the recommendation.

**2. Gross margin, the strategy document's central finding, is absent from the recommendation.**
`value-capture.md` TL;DR: "A harness that rents frontier models resells them at a loss or a thin spread"; §3: "only the layer that makes them has a structural margin." Lindy, a 25-person company, moved 100% of inference to DeepSeek V4 because "inference had exceeded payroll" (`non-technical-startups-enterprise.md` §4). The recommendation says "Rent the engine" and its "Risks the plan carries" lists liability, bundling, timing and evidence quality. Margin is not on the list. Worse, the outer harness is a token multiplier on rented tokens: the classifier "adds 'a round-trip before execution,' billed on Enterprise and API accounts"; agent teams "use approximately 7x more tokens" (`runtime-and-safety.md` §10); "a styled page is more token-intensive than the same content as terminal text" (`docs/05-surfaces/browser-generated-ui.md` §2.1); Anthropic's own Code Review "averages 20 minutes and $15–25" per review (`docs/06-users/developers.md` §4.3). A second-model review, a consequence classifier, a generated proof-of-work page and one subagent per vendor, on top of the engine, is the cost structure of a company that loses more per task the better its product works.

**3. Microsoft and GitHub already ship cross-vendor governance, and the labs are routing "policy travels with the person" to the identity provider.**
The recommendation's defense is that "Neutrality is structurally against the labs' interest" and that Agent 365 covers only "Microsoft's surfaces." The corpus says otherwise: Agent 365 discovers "shadow agents" "starting with OpenClaw 'and expanding soon to ... GitHub Copilot CLI and Claude Code'" (`non-technical-labs.md`, Microsoft section); Intune policies "can be used to block common execution methods for OpenClaw agents" (`os-level.md` §2.1); GitHub's Agent HQ runs "Claude, Codex and Copilot 'from a single interface'" and "Copilot CLI defaults to a Claude model"; Copilot Cowork "runs on Anthropic models" (`developer-harnesses-labs.md`). Anthropic's enterprise-managed MCP auth hands connector authorization to "the organization's identity provider." The neutral control plane is not unoccupied; it is occupied by the company that owns the identity, the directory, the office suite and the data graph, and the labs are feeding it.

**4. The academic and practitioner documents argue against generating the inner harness per task.**
`docs/04-research/academic.md` §4: HarnessDev finds generated harnesses "remain substantially behind mature human-engineered references on code and on search and research" and evolution gains "are unstable and transfer only partially to held-out tasks"; §6: harness evolution "averaged 67.4 pass@1 versus 72.3 for parallel sampling and 68.2 for direct sampling." `industry-engineering.md` §5: Manus, "the KV-cache hit rate is the single most important metric for a production-stage AI agent," with cached input at a tenth of uncached cost; the "re-simplify on model upgrades" advice means remove harness, not regenerate it. A per-task generated prompt, tool subset and workflow is the design that maximizes cache misses and minimizes the postmortem baseline (item 7 above). The commercial examples the design cites (n8n Assistant, Ghostwriter, Invent) build a workflow once from a description; none regenerates and discards per run.

**5. Memory is an injection sink, and the audience that would own a portable store rejects proprietary stores.**
`runtime-and-safety.md` §5: "a successful prompt injection could write malicious content into the store. Later sessions then read that content as trusted memory"; "No independent study links a memory system to better real-task completion"; shared stores need "`read_only` defaults." A memory that travels across vendors is a poisoning that travels across vendors, and the design's part 3 says nothing about it. `developers.md` §7: developers refuse to give up "plain-text configuration in git" and "local execution with secrets staying local," and "a surface that hides project state in a proprietary store will be rejected by the people it targets."

**6. The entry surface the recommendation chooses is the one the corpus says cannot separate the requester from the room.**
`messaging.md` §8: "No product distinguishes, inside a thread, the requester's instructions from bystanders' text." §7: Anthropic's Slack doc warns "Claude may follow directions from other messages in the context, so users should make sure to only use Claude in trusted Slack conversations"; Claude Code in Slack "does not work in direct messages"; "The approval message itself is an injection surface." The design's walkthrough starts "in the Slack channel where the finance team works." The recommendation's prototype has "one entry surface (Slack or Teams)." Neither mentions the warning.

**7. The reviewer the design relies on cannot review.**
`non-technical.md` §3.1 (CHI 2025, primary): verification fails when people "lack the domain expertise to spot errors. The last is the non-technical user's condition"; the Plan-Then-Execute study found "plausible yet flawed plans earn misplaced confidence." `developers.md` §4.2 quotes Anthropic's own docs: "After the tenth approval you're clicking through rather than reviewing." The design's proof-of-work page assumes a person who reads evidence; the corpus's evidence about that person says a well-formatted page increases confidence more than accuracy.

**8. The spreadsheet and the office add-ins are the verification surface that already works, and the design ignores them.**
Finding 10 flags it ("Spreadsheets may be closer to a non-technical harness than any generated page"); the design then proposes a generated HTML page with "spreadsheet-grade provenance" that `browser-generated-ui.md` §3.3 says "no HTML surface copies." Meanwhile "Agentic Word/Excel/PowerPoint went GA April 22, 2026 with +52%/+67%/+11% weekly engagement" and Anthropic's Excel, Word and PowerPoint add-ins went GA May 7, 2026 (`non-technical-labs.md` TL;DR). The one surface where a non-expert can already check an agent's work cell by cell is treated as an open question rather than as the design's default review surface.

**9. Enforcement points are missing on the runtimes the design says to rent.**
`developer-harnesses-labs.md`, Qualification one: cloud sessions drop "LSP plugins, `/plugin`, `bypassPermissions`" and agent teams; `protocols.md` §8: "hooks do not yet travel cleanly from a laptop to a hosted surface"; `anatomy.md` §3.5: the sandbox "restricts only Bash commands. Built-in file tools, MCP servers, and hooks still run directly on your host"; Managed Agents: "Anthropic runs the agent and the sandbox," "not currently eligible for Zero Data Retention" and, per the labs verification notes, not eligible for a HIPAA BAA. The design's "capability scoping at the read boundary" needs a place to stand between the engine's built-in tools and the data; on a rented engine that place belongs to the vendor. And a finance wedge on a runtime without ZDR is a procurement problem the recommendation never raises.

**10. The free outer-harness supply is exploding, from the labs themselves.**
`history.md` Era 4: DeepSeek Harness, MIT, "everything is a plugin" with "models, tools, skills, sessions, sandboxes, loops, scheduling and the UI all swappable," 216k stars 26 days after creation; Hermes at 243k with "Sandboxed runtimes" and an A2A endpoint; Kiro Crew, open source, persisting "sessions, memory, schedules and checkpoints," reachable from seven chat apps (`developer-harnesses-independent.md`, `developer-harnesses-labs.md`). The design's "adapters to several engines" is a plugin to any of these; none of them appears in the synthesis.

**11. The buyer can now build the outer harness instead of buying it.**
`adoption-evidence.md` §5: McKinsey 2026, "32% decided against purchasing at least one software product or feature because they could build it in-house with agentic coding tools (confirmed)." Microsoft's Cowork "core team ... growing from three engineers at the start to just nine"; Anthropic says Cowork was built "in about a week and a half with Claude Code (vendor claim)"; OpenAI shipped "roughly one million lines" with three engineers. An authority layer made of hooks, a classifier call, a ledger table and a Slack relay is the archetype of the thing an enterprise IT team vibe-codes in a quarter.

**12. Cost is the first-named reason agent projects get cancelled.**
`non-technical.md` §3.4: "Gartner names 'escalating costs' first among cancellation reasons," in a forecast that "over 40% of agentic projects" will be cancelled by end-2027; Copilot Cowork ships "off by default" with per-task prices shown. The design's Principle 9 ("Cost is a first-class control") is right; the design's architecture is the most expensive one in the corpus.

## Attacks on the design

**1. Consequence is a property of the action in context, not of the tool, so the classes will not hold.**
"Send an email" is reversible to a colleague and irreversible to a vendor; "create draft correction entry" in an accounting system is reversible in the system and catastrophic in effect if the correction changes a vendor's bank account before the next payment run. A class tagged on the tool cannot know this; a class assigned per action needs a model to assign it, and the corpus says that model can be fooled: the classifier "is a per-action control, not an isolation boundary"; narrow allow rules can let "a destructive argument through without the classifier seeing it"; a stated boundary "can be lost if context compaction removes the message that stated it" (`runtime-and-safety.md` §6, §7). The design ends with a second permission vocabulary (reversible, compensable, irreversible, ceiling, grant) laid over six modes, four policies and Dust's four stakes, readable by nobody the design is for.

**2. The undo ledger does nothing at the one moment the wedge is defined by.**
The recommendation's wedge criterion is "a native checkpoint (a draft before a send, a hold before a release)," and the AP candidate's checkpoint is "the payment release." A released payment is irreversible; the ledger records "the compensating action and who to notify," which is a phone call to the vendor and an insurance claim. `messaging.md` §9: "the only reversible boundary is a PR or an email draft." The design's own open question concedes "Compensating actions are not undo." So the ledger's value is highest for the actions that need it least (drafts) and zero for the action the whole wedge is sold on, while the promise of a ledger creates a duty ("who to notify") that a startup will be held to.

**3. The operations-manager walkthrough is business email compromise with an agent doing the typing.**
The agent reads vendor emails (untrusted content), holds accounting and purchasing data (private data) and drafts emails to vendors (external communication). That is all three legs of the lethal trifecta the runtime document says must be broken per task: "remove one leg and the attack fails"; "Break the trifecta per task; allowlist egress automatically" (§8, §11). The design's defense, "nothing it reads can widen what it may do," defends against the wrong attack. An injected vendor email does not need wider permissions; it needs the agent to draft a plausible "correction" or "vendor reply" inside its grant, and it needs the manager to click approve, which the corpus says she does 93% of the time and cannot evaluate when she does not (Evidence items 6 and 7). Add that the request arrived through a Slack channel every colleague can post in, and the walkthrough is a threat model, not a product demo.

**4. On a rented engine the outer harness is either advisory or an engine in disguise.**
The design "never owns a loop" and enforces policy "at the read boundary." Built-in tools (bash, file, browser, computer use) on Claude Code, Codex and Managed Agents do not pass through a third party's MCP gateway; hooks are absent on hosted runtimes (Evidence item 9). To enforce consequence classes on every action the harness must either proxy every tool, own the sandbox and inject the prompt, which is a loop by another name, or express its policy as text the engine may ignore, which `anatomy.md` calls "context, not enforced configuration." The design does not say which, and the difference is the product.

**5. The cost model is inverted: the safer the design, the more it spends per task.**
Every safety piece is a model call or a re-read: a classifier round-trip per action (billed), a second-model review, a generated page, a fresh-context evaluator, a dispatcher model for interruptions, and workflow fan-out (7x tokens). Per-task regeneration of the inner harness defeats prompt caching (Evidence item 4). Anthropic's price for one proof-of-work review of code is $15–25; the recommendation's only public outcome price is $0.99 per resolution. Nobody in the corpus has made an authority layer this thick pay for itself, and the design offers no estimate.

**6. People will not use the controls the way the design needs them to.**
The design's approval regime depends on a person who reads consequence read-backs, batches wisely and undoes promptly. The record: 93% of prompts approved and 13.6% of dangerous commands caught (vendor figures, from a draft document); "after the tenth approval you're clicking through"; screen-less confirmation is "unsolved" and the voice document's only working pattern is "say it, then tap it"; the winning non-technical archetype (Sierra, Decagon, Agentforce) works because "a non-technical operator edits policy text while the vendor owns the loop" and never sees a permission model (`non-technical-startups-enterprise.md`, Nuance). An "approve-all for the reversible ones" button trains the reflex the design is trying to break.

**7. The liability lands on the smallest company in the chain.**
Whoever holds the grants, the credentials proxy and the ledger holds the blast radius; the recommendation admits this in one line and moves on. The incumbents that shipped injection flaws (ServiceNow's second-order injection and BodySnatcher, Microsoft's EchoLeak, SearchLeak and CoSnitch) survived because they own the paper and the data; Replit's CEO called its deletion "unacceptable and should never be possible" and shipped harness fixes from a position of $9B. A startup holding payment-release authority for mid-sized companies on a runtime with no ZDR, no BAA and a five-times-changed billing policy is the party an auditor, an insurer and a plaintiff will find first.

**8. Portable memory owned by the person is a portable injection vector and an enterprise non-starter.**
Evidence item 5. In an organization the "person's" memory of policies and past decisions is the organization's record; the owner is the tenant, and the tenant's owner is the identity provider and the suite. The design's memory store would be a third copy of data that already lives in the CRM, the ticketing system and the mailbox, with a new write path from an agent that reads the open web.

**9. The interruption policy adds a fifth judgment model to a loop the vendors are already absorbing.**
`anatomy.md` §5: "the judgment parts of a harness are being replaced by models (compactor, classifier, evaluator, dreamer), while enforcement and plumbing remain code. A harness startup's durable ground is the second set." The design's "small model of presence, urgency and reversibility" is judgment, sitting on top of vendors' push toggles and presence files, justified by one retired product with an unstated cause (Findings item 11). It will be a Claude Code setting within a year.

**10. The "stable" outer harness is a compensation layer for everything except identity and audit.**
The recommendation's own timing risk: "a layer designed for today's failure modes must be re-simplified as often as an inner harness." Consequence classes exist because today's models cannot be trusted to send email; read-backs exist because today's models mis-scope; the interruption model exists because today's agents nag. Those are exactly the "assumptions that expire" the industry document warns about. What does not expire is who the agent is, what it may reach and what it did: identity, scoping and audit. The design should be one third its current size, and the two thirds it should drop are the parts it calls new.

## Attacks on the recommendation

**1. It inverts the strategy document's own ranking.**
`value-capture.md` §11 verdicts: the open runtime and control plane is "weak as a venture-scale business unless it owns the control plane and the API keys"; the vertical harness is "the most defensible position, and the least 'harness-shaped'." The recommendation makes the vertical the wedge and the control plane "the company." The document it cites for the ranking says to do the reverse, and gives the reason: revenue in that layer "means becoming the enterprise's governance layer, against GitHub, Microsoft and the labs' enterprise tiers, while the lab can reclassify your traffic at will."

**2. "The labs move slower in this layer" is false on the corpus's own timeline.**
The recommendation: "The labs ship the runtime weekly; the authority layer for non-developers moves slower and inconsistently." From the corpus, April to September 2026: Cowork GA with RBAC, group spend limits and per-tool connector controls (Apr 9); Agent 365 GA at $15 per user (May 1); Copilot Studio computer-use GA (May 26); Scout with its own Entra identity, human sign-off and in-the-moment DLP (Jun 2); Copilot Cowork GA, off by default, per-task credits, tenant/group/user caps (Jun 16); approval-message anti-spoofing (Jul 15) and trust-gated relay (Aug 17) in Claude Code; auto mode default (Aug 14); enterprise-managed MCP auth (Aug 24); Claude in Chrome GA with a per-action classifier (Aug 26); "always ask you first" before reading others' artifacts (Sep 1); a Windows 365 for Agents MCP server GA (Sep 2). Divergent, yes. Slow, no. A company "that does only this" is racing three vendors who each shipped more of it in one quarter than the recommendation's prototype plan contains.

**3. The Okta analogy fails because the platform owner here already owns identity and already ships neutrality.**
Identity providers existed because operating systems and SaaS vendors did not want to be each other's directory. Microsoft is the directory (Entra), the office suite, the collaboration surface (Teams), the OS, and the vendor of cross-vendor agent governance (Evidence item 3), and it runs its flagship non-technical agent on a rival's models. The labs are pushing connector authorization into the IdP. The "neutral" seat is taken by the least neutral company in the corpus, and the tenant already pays it.

**4. The plan's two halves have opposite exits.**
The recommendation lists "Bundling" as a risk: "The suites buy the best standalone agents; the exit may be the plan." The corpus shows the suites doing exactly that (Fin $3.6B, Moveworks $2.85B, Veza ~$1.25B, Poke to Cognition, SAP into n8n). A vertical wedge's natural exit is a suite; a suite acquirer wants one vendor's stack and pays nothing for cross-vendor portability. So the wedge is worth most without the layer, and the layer is worth most without the wedge. The recommendation calls that sequencing a virtue.

**5. The wedge criteria select for markets where the startup is a feature.**
Criteria 4 and 5 ("Data already behind connectors," "A buyer who already pays for governance") describe markets where an incumbent already holds the data and the governance budget. Criterion 1 is a category error (Findings item 9). Missing criterion: nobody already owns the checkpoint. Apply it: accounts payable's checkpoint (the payment release) belongs to the finance system and the bank, the corpus contains no research on that market at all, and "blast radius is money" is the liability attack in a sentence; sales operations' checkpoint belongs to Salesforce, which has Agentforce at $1.2B ARR and is buying Fin; service-desk access grants belong to ServiceNow (Moveworks, Veza, Armis) and to the identity vendors, so "identity is the product here" means fighting Entra and Okta on identity. The recommendation flags two of these as "the risk" and proposes them anyway; the third it does not flag.

**6. Falsifier 2 is already the corpus's answer.**
"Interviews find that non-technical operators do not want to see consequence classes or ledgers at all, only outcomes." The operator-configured archetype wins precisely because operators "edit policy text while the vendor owns the loop" and never see a permission model; the 93% approval rate says people click through whatever is shown; Microsoft's plan-checkpoint-approve script reached "more than half of the Fortune 500" without a consequence vocabulary. Twenty interviews will confirm what the documents already say, and the recommendation's response ("the layer is invisible plumbing and the company is the vertical alone") is the alternative thesis it should have started from.

**7. The developer half of the brief is dropped, along with the corpus's best-supported opening.**
The brief asks for a harness "optimised for developers and for non-technical people." The design's developer walkthrough is what the labs ship today (worktrees, fleet view, phone approvals, Code Review, merge blocked by auto mode); the recommendation then chooses a non-technical wedge only. `developers.md`, Nuance: "The defensible position for an outsider is verification and proof of work across any engine, the one thing neither the terminal nor the current managers do, and where DORA and Faros say value leaks." That is a buyer with a budget and a countable unit, ignored in favour of a market the corpus never studied.

**8. "Portable across engines from the first day" is a tax paid before any customer asks for it.**
`developer-harnesses-labs.md`: "Cross-vendor runtimes exist; cross-vendor engines do not"; feature sets fork per runtime; `industry-engineering.md`: harness quality is model-coupled and an abstraction over models "may be structurally worse than each lab's own." Lindy's switch to DeepSeek was one cut-over after six to nine months of re-engineering, a migration, not a runtime feature. Customers change engines wholesale and rarely; building three adapters on day one buys demo value and no revenue.

**9. The eval is not a moat.**
"An evaluation that does not exist yet ... Publish it; nobody measures this." Zapier already publishes AutomationBench across six business functions; HAL, the cost-controlled leaderboard, was archived in July 2026; OpenAI bought Promptfoo. Publishing an evaluation is marketing, and marketing that helps the labs tune against it.

**10. The buyer can build it.**
Evidence item 11: 32% of enterprises skipped buying at least one product because agentic coding let them build it; the outer harness as described is hooks, a classifier call, a ledger table, a Slack relay and a page template. The recommendation's answer to "why would a lab not just do this" never asks "why would the customer not just do this."

**11. Managed Agents is the wrong substrate for the wedge it is paired with.**
The prototype plan names "the Claude Agent SDK, Codex's app-server and Managed Agents." Managed Agents is not ZDR-eligible and not BAA-eligible; the Agent SDK's terms forbid third parties from offering claude.ai login or rate limits; the subscription route was closed five times in eight months. A finance wedge for mid-sized companies on that substrate fails procurement before it fails the market.

**12. A skeptical investor's four questions, in the strategy document's own words.**
"Who holds the meter, and what does the customer lose by switching?" The lab; nothing. "Can you price and package to value and outcomes?" (Sequoia) Only in the wedge; the destination is seats-plus-credits, the model the strategy document says is squeezed from both sides. Which of data, workflow, collaboration or a vertical do you own at the destination? None. Who is the comparable? OpenHands, an enterprise control plane on an open harness, raised about $24M. The investor will conclude that the wedge is the company and the layer is a slide.

**Where the recommendation is right.** Do not build an engine; do not build a new surface; treat the general position as earned, not assumed; measure approval load, regret, time to proof and cost per task. Those four numbers are the best sentence in the three documents.

## The strongest alternative thesis

Sell verification, not authority. Build the independent verifier.

Every finding the coordinator grades "strong" points at one gap the labs leave open: nobody checks the work from outside. For developers, review is the bottleneck: review time is up 91% in the only telemetry cited, DORA finds throughput up and stability down, and OpenAI's own figure is three to five sessions per engineer. For everyone else, the work cannot be checked at all: "a strategy memo doesn't compile," and verification fails when people "lack the domain expertise to spot errors." The labs sell generation and bundle a self-review that "never blocks merging." A check from the same vendor, model family and context as the work it checks is not independent; Qodo raised $70M on that pitch for code. The design document's own table says the nearest thing for documents, spreadsheets and records is "Nothing."

Concretely: a second-party verification service delivered as an MCP server and a Stop hook into every harness (Claude Code, Codex, Cowork, Copilot, the open harnesses) and as an add-in in Excel, Word and Outlook, which went GA in April and May 2026. It takes the task, the artifact and the trace and returns proof: which claims rest on which source rows, which checks ran, what changed, what could not be verified, rendered into the grid, the one surface a non-expert can read cell by cell. It never acts, holds no credentials, keeps no ledger and owns no surface, so it inherits none of the liability, the identity fight, the trifecta or the platform risk the coordinator's layer carries. A check reads rather than fans out, so it is cheap relative to generation. It is model-neutral by construction, because a verifier has to be, and buyers accept a neutral checker where they distrust a neutral controller. Its unit is countable (a verified pull request, reconciliation or report), so it prices per outcome from day one.

Wedge: developers, because they have a budget, a countable unit and hooks that exist today. Second market: the office suite, through the add-ins. The moat is the corpus of verified-versus-unverified artifacts: the evaluation the coordinator says nobody has (Finding 9), produced as a by-product rather than a research program. Why a lab will not do it: a first-party verifier has a conflict of interest, the labs' own reviewers are built not to block, and verification is the one judgment part that must not be absorbed into the model that produced the work. It beats the pure vertical because it needs no sales force in two hundred verticals, rides on settled surfaces and engines, and is a product one engineer can build.

It gives up the reimagined default harness deliberately, because the corpus says the default is the chat app and the office suite, both owned. It keeps Principle 4 (verification is a product), Principle 10 (own the eval) and the four numbers.
