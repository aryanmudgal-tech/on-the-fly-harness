# What the evidence says: eleven findings and ten principles

*Research program: agent harnesses. Synthesis written 2026-09-08 by the coordinator from the nineteen research documents in this repository; revised 2026-09-09 after the red-team review in `04-red-team.md`. Status: final. Every finding cites the documents it rests on; the documents carry the sources and their verification notes.*

## What this document answers

- Which parts of the thesis "as models get better, the harness becomes the bottleneck, and neither a CLI nor a desktop app can be the default way people operate agents" survive contact with the evidence, and which do not.
- What the evidence says a reimagined harness has to do, stated as principles that the design (`02-reimagined-harness.md`) and the recommendation (`03-recommendation.md`) build on.

## How to read the citations

Square brackets name a research document: [anatomy] and [history] are the primers in `01-primer/`; [labs], [independent], [matrix], [nontech-labs], [nontech-startups] and [adoption] are in `02-landscape/`; [protocols] and [runtime] in `03-building-blocks/`; [academic] and [industry] in `04-research/`; [messaging], [generated-ui], [voice] and [os] in `05-surfaces/`; [dev-users] and [nontech-users] in `06-users/`; [strategy] in `07-strategy/`. "Vendor" after a number means the figure was reported by the company that benefits from it. Many 2026 figures were verified through search excerpts and GitHub-hosted mirrors because this session's network policy blocked most primary sites; each document lists what could not be opened. Where two documents disagree, this synthesis says so and prefers the one with the primary fetch.

## What changed after the red team

The first version of this document was more confident than its sources in four places, and the red team caught them: it reported the friendlier of two accounts of Anthropic's third-party policy; it called the binding constraint for non-technical users "authority, not accuracy" when a sibling document says reliability binds too; it called the inner harness "trivial" when its own sources record double-digit swings from inner-harness configuration; and it called several design pieces new that Dust, Microsoft and the MCP specification already ship. Those are corrected below, and the principles changed with them: the inner harness is the vendor's, not a startup's, and verification moved from one principle among ten to the centre.

## The thesis, scored

| Clause of the thesis | Verdict | Where the evidence is |
|---|---|---|
| The harness decides whether agents work | Supported, with a split: the outer layer (identity, permissions, verification, memory, cost, surfaces) is where incidents, spend and value sit; the inner layer (loop, prompts, scaffolds) is small in lines but not in leverage, and it belongs to whoever owns the model | Findings 1, 9 |
| Better models make the harness the bottleneck | Half right: better models absorb inner-harness compensations and expose the outer layer; reliability itself is still model-side and still binding for business tasks | Findings 1, 4, 6 |
| Neither a CLI nor a desktop app can be the default surface | Half right: the CLI became the engine, not the front door, and chat surfaces carry two orders of magnitude more people; but the desktop app as a client of a cloud runtime is exactly where the two largest labs converged in 2026 | Findings 2, 3 |
| The default harness must be reimagined for non-technical people | True for the trust model, not the surface, and the observed demand is thin, attended and document-shaped, which constrains what "reimagined" may mean | Findings 4, 5, 10 |
| A startup can own the reimagined default | Not for a general harness, and not for a neutral control plane either, because that seat is held by the company that owns identity and the office suite; the openings are verification and vertical operator-configured harnesses | Findings 7, 8 |

## Eleven findings

### 1. The harness is two layers that move in opposite directions, and the inner one belongs to the model's owner

The inner layer is small in lines. A code-editing agent fits in a few hundred lines [industry]; pi ships four tools and a system prompt of about 300 words and is the engine under OpenClaw [independent]; mini-SWE-agent, about 100 lines with bash as its only tool, reports over 74% on SWE-bench Verified [academic]; METR found in February 2026 that the Claude Code and Codex product scaffolds did not outperform its own simple scaffolds [academic]. Anthropic tells builders that every harness component "assumes the model can't do something; those assumptions expire" and to "re-simplify on model upgrades" [industry]. Whole functions have moved into vendor APIs: compaction became a request header, memory a server-side store, permission prompts a classifier, voice turn-taking an API parameter [anatomy][runtime][voice].

Small in lines is not small in leverage. LangChain moved from 52.8% to 66.5% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with the same model by changing its harness; Anthropic's April 2026 postmortem traced a Claude Code regression to a reasoning-effort default, a caching bug and a verbosity prompt, with the model unchanged [industry]; independent papers in mid-2026 report double-digit swings for the same model under different harnesses [history]. The practitioner document's conclusion is that a harness "for everyone" that abstracts over models "may be structurally worse than each lab's own" [industry].

The outer layer thickens and is where the labs spent 2026: sandboxes, credential vaults, checkpointing, scheduling, webhooks, agent identities, cost meters and classifier-gated permissions [labs][runtime]. Microsoft's answer to non-technical agents was a runtime, per-agent identities in its directory, per-task credits and an approval loop [nontech-labs]. Every incident in the corpus lives in this layer [runtime][os][messaging].

**So what.** "Better models need better harnesses" is right about the outer layer. The inner layer is model-coupled: use the vendor's tested default, re-simplify on upgrades, and never make it a moat. That also rules out regenerating it per task, which maximizes cache misses and discards the tested baseline that postmortems depend on [industry][academic].

### 2. "One runtime, many surfaces" is the settled shape, and the CLI became the engine

Anthropic's documentation states that each surface connects to the same underlying Claude Code engine, across terminal, IDEs, desktop, web, mobile, Slack, Chrome, Remote Control and messaging channels [labs]. Codex is one Rust core under a CLI, IDE extensions, a desktop app and a cloud service; GitHub's Copilot SDK talks to the same engine behind the Copilot CLI; Amazon's Kiro drives its CLI over the Agent Client Protocol [labs]. Cloud environments apply wherever a session starts [runtime]. Among independents, every orchestrator wraps a CLI, and the Agent Client Protocol standardizes "agent as a subprocess" [independent]. New harnesses still launch terminal-first: Meta's Muse Code in August 2026 [history]. The matrix qualifies the pattern twice: feature sets fork by execution location (cloud sessions drop plugins, LSP and some permission modes), and GitHub is building the inverse, one surface for many engines [matrix][labs].

**So what.** The author's assumption that the CLI cannot be the default is already the industry's position for the surface and wrong for the engine. A startup harness that rebuilds the engine competes with engines the labs give away (Codex, Mistral Vibe), rent (Managed Agents at tokens plus $0.08 per session-hour) or open-source at scale (DeepSeek Harness reached about 216k stars in 26 days; Hermes about 243k; Kiro's Crew is open) [labs][runtime][history][independent].

### 3. Standalone new surfaces have the worst record in the corpus; embedded surfaces have the revenue

Folded, sold, stalled or shut within roughly a year: OpenAI's Operator (folded into ChatGPT within six months) and Atlas browser (292 days) [os][nontech-labs]; Google's Project Mariner (shut May 2026) [nontech-labs]; Humane (dead), Rabbit (survived by hosting other people's agents) [voice]; Dia (bought for $610M in 2025, still single-platform a year later), Manus (bounced between owners after a government order), Poke (sold four months after public launch), Convergence's Proxy (absorbed into Salesforce) [nontech-startups]; OpenAI's visual Agent Builder (scheduled to close on November 30, 2026, with non-technical agent building moved into ChatGPT as Workspace Agents rather than abandoned) [labs]; Terragon, Vibe Kanban, Roo Code's extension and Continue on the developer side [independent].

Where the revenue is: Agentforce at $1.2B ARR, ServiceNow's AI contract value past $1B, Glean past $300M, Microsoft 365 Copilot at 30M paid seats, Copilot Cowork used by more than half the Fortune 500 during a three-month preview (all vendor figures) [nontech-startups][nontech-labs][messaging]. Those live in a CRM, a service console, a search bar, Slack, Teams and the office suite.

By people and messages, chat wins by two orders of magnitude: ChatGPT reported about 1B weekly users in mid-2026 against roughly 10M combined users for Codex and ChatGPT Work (vendor statements via secondary reports) [adoption]. By money and delegated autonomy, developer surfaces lead: coding took roughly 55% of departmental enterprise AI spend in 2025 (Menlo Ventures, via secondary); the Economic Index shows automation-style conversations at 60.8% among those matched to software-developer tasks against 48.6% overall [adoption]. The fastest-growing cohort inside the coding agents is non-developers (vendor, via secondary) [adoption]. The desktop app is not dead: the two largest labs converged on a desktop app as the non-terminal client of a cloud runtime, and Codex's merge into the ChatGPT app coincided with its fastest growth [adoption][nontech-labs].

**So what.** For non-technical people the default surface is wherever the data and identity already are: chat inside existing tools, the office suite, the OS assistant, a phone notification, with a desktop or web app as the window to review in. What survived was reimagining the trust model inside existing surfaces, not the body style.

### 4. Non-technical delegation is thin, attended and document-shaped; authority and reliability both bind

Seventy-six percent of Claude chat and Cowork conversations match tasks outside computing; the top matched tasks are searching for information, reference questions, product advice and editing [nontech-users]. ChatGPT use is about 70% non-work, and two-thirds of writing requests edit text the user supplied [nontech-users]. Conversations matched to clerical tasks run 69 to 75% automation-style against 31 to 38% for those matched to lawyers, credit counselors and marketing managers, but the dataset matches conversation content to tasks, not people to jobs, and it records what people type into a chat window, not what authority they grant [nontech-users][adoption]. Workers want automation for 46.1% of tasks but "equal partnership" in 45.2% of occupations [nontech-users]. Both user documents warn that an unattended multi-application agent may be ahead of demand; the thin harness (chat plus retrieval plus a document) is where usage sits [nontech-labs][nontech-users].

Two constraints bind. Authority: students regret agents that "acted beyond what they would have authorized"; Replit's agent deleted a production database during a declared code freeze; OpenClaw, the one developer-shaped harness that reached consumers at scale, produced the category's worst incident record [nontech-users][runtime][messaging]. Reliability: the best model passes 50.3% of Zapier's 600 business tasks; an independent test of Cowork, Lindy, Sauna and Opal scored the best about 1.25 out of 3 and failed them on memory, inspectable artifacts and compounding context [nontech-startups]. The startups document's line is the whole problem in one sentence: "Code has a test suite. A strategy memo doesn't compile." And the person who is supposed to check the work often cannot: verification fails when people "lack the domain expertise to spot errors", and plausible but flawed plans earn misplaced confidence [nontech-users].

**So what.** Reliability is model-side and the labs are moving it. The harness-side problems for this audience are the ability to check work without expertise, permissions expressed as consequences, memory across tools and cost in dollars. The surface is settled: chat inside existing tools, a cloud runtime, a plan with checkpoints, a notification, a review [nontech-labs][nontech-users].

### 5. Approvals are divergent across vendors, and policy is centralizing in the identity provider

Seven vendors show seven approval models, and none shows the user a consistent picture of what an action can cost or destroy [nontech-labs]. Approval fatigue is measured: users approved most prompts (93% or 97% depending on the report) and humans caught 13.6% of dangerous commands against 89% for a classifier (vendor figures via secondary) [runtime]; Anthropic's classifier-gated auto mode became the default for new Claude Code sessions on paid plans on August 14, 2026 [anatomy][runtime]. Approvals inside chat are text-grade: a relayed prompt answered with "yes" plus a code [messaging].

Approval design is not absent, it is scattered. Dust tags every MCP tool with a stake (never ask, low, medium, high) that maps to review expectations, with external tools defaulting to high [nontech-startups]; MCP's specification carries read-only and destructive tool annotations that its maintainers call a "risk vocabulary" [protocols]; Microsoft's Scout runs under its own directory identity with human sign-off for sensitive actions and data-loss prevention enforced in the moment [nontech-labs]; Windows runs agents in a separate account and session [os]; Claude Desktop's computer use has fixed tiers and an escape key that injection cannot dismiss [os]; AP2 carries signed intent, cart and payment mandates [protocols]; OpenClaw's chat approvals can only tighten configured policy [messaging]; Copilot Cowork ships off by default with per-task prices and tenant, group and user caps [nontech-labs]; Teams' approve-before-sharing is the one new primitive in chat [messaging].

Policy is moving into the organization's identity provider: Cowork went GA with role-based access, group spend limits and per-tool connector controls; Anthropic's enterprise-managed MCP authorization (August 2026) puts connector authorization in the identity provider; managed policy hooks cannot be disabled by users [nontech-labs][protocols]. What does not travel is per-person policy across vendors and surfaces: user-local settings stay on the laptop and hosted surfaces ignore them [protocols].

**So what.** The gap is cross-vendor and per-person, and the natural owner of that gap is the identity provider, which today means Microsoft. A startup should not expect to own approvals; it can expect to read them, which is what verification needs.

### 6. Security failures are structural and cluster where the agent reads untrusted content

Prompt injection is unsolved on every vendor's admission [runtime][os]. The 2026 incident list runs through calendar invites, browser extensions, support bots, enterprise search and a VM escape [nontech-labs]; OpenClaw published roughly 650 advisories in five months, many of them authorization bypasses in message handling [messaging]. The defenses that hold are architectural: an OS boundary, an egress proxy that keeps credentials outside the boundary, a second model that reviews actions, and breaking the "lethal trifecta" (private data, untrusted content, external communication) per task [runtime]. A memory store is an injection sink: a successful injection can write into it and later sessions read it as trusted [runtime]. Sandboxing itself is a commodity with a dozen interchangeable suppliers [runtime]. On hosted engines a third party cannot interpose on built-in tools, and hooks do not yet travel from a laptop to a hosted surface, so an outside layer that wants to enforce policy is either advisory or must own the sandbox [labs][protocols][anatomy].

**So what.** A product for non-technical people must be safe by structure, and a product that does not hold credentials or act at all inherits none of this liability. That asymmetry favours checking over controlling.

### 7. The labs hold the meter, rewrite the terms, and value goes to whoever owns the outcome, the data or the channel

Cursor's gross margin was reported at minus 23% for the quarter ending January 2026 and turned positive only after it shipped its own models and repriced; Lindy moved all inference to DeepSeek because inference had exceeded payroll [strategy][nontech-startups]. Anthropic's terms for third-party harnesses changed at least five times in eight months: a block on April 4, 2026; a reinstatement behind monthly Agent SDK credits announced May 13 for June 15; that change paused on June 15, with usage back on plan limits; from July 13, third-party apps drawing on "extra usage" instead of plan limits; and by September 3, detection of OpenClaw's own prompt markers leaving subscription users of the largest third-party harness without a model unless they buy extra-usage credit [strategy]. Within six months of 2026, four standalone developer harnesses died or were absorbed; the survivors own a model (Cursor, Cognition), a workflow (Factory, Qodo) or a builder surface for non-developers (Lovable, Replit) [independent]. The year's large deals went to owners of distribution and compute (SpaceX and Cursor, Salesforce and Fin, SAP's stake in n8n), with one harness company buying another (Cognition and Poke) [strategy][nontech-startups]. The labs are absorbing the runtime: Managed Agents bundles loop, sandbox, memory, tracing and scheduling behind one API, without zero-data-retention eligibility [runtime][labs]. And buyers can now build: 32% of organizations skipped buying at least one software product because agentic coding tools let them build it in-house (McKinsey, via secondary) [adoption].

**So what.** A general-purpose harness is not a venture-scale business on this evidence, and neither is anything that resells rented tokens with a thin spread. The strategy document's own ranking puts the vertical operator-configured harness first and the neutral control plane last unless it owns the keys [strategy].

### 8. Protocols separated surface from runtime and commoditized the thick features; the labs own the tool and agent protocols, not the interface ones

MCP won the tool layer (about half a billion SDK downloads a month, vendor), moved to the Linux Foundation, and in July 2026 dropped sessions and the initialization handshake to become stateless HTTP [protocols]. Instruction files, skills, hooks and plugin manifests are near-identical across Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Cursor [protocols][matrix]. The tool and agent protocols (MCP, A2A, the Apps SDK, A2UI, AP2, UCP, AGENTS.md, Agent Skills) were created by Anthropic, OpenAI or Google [protocols]. The interface-side protocols were not: the Agent Client Protocol is Zed's with JetBrains as co-lead and puts 41 agents behind one editor interface; AG-UI is CopilotKit's; the generative-UI layer in the AI SDK is Vercel's; and those put the agent inside an interface a third party owns [protocols]. Hooks exist in every major harness with near-identical event names, which is the cheapest place for an outside party to observe every action [protocols][matrix].

**So what.** A startup inherits the plugs but not the traffic. The interface-side protocols and the hooks are good news for a cross-engine layer that reads; the lab-owned protocols decide where third-party UI is delivered, which is into lab-owned chat apps.

### 9. Automated harness design is real, overfits, and the layer that matters has no benchmark of the operator's own work

Harness design is now a search problem with working automation, from workflow search in 2024 to self-rewriting coding agents in 2025 to whole-harness optimization in 2026 [academic]. The best independent evidence is unfavourable: harness evolution does not consistently beat plain test-time scaling at matched budgets and transfers about 0.6 points to held-out tasks; generated harnesses "remain substantially behind mature human-engineered references" [academic]. METR's doubling time for software-task horizons is 89 to 131 days depending on the window [academic]. Business-task benchmarks exist (Zapier's AutomationBench across six functions; Sierra's tau-bench, whose 2024 airline figure of 22.5% pass-four-times has been superseded by 2026 telecom successors reporting around 99%, likely on lenient averaging), but the cost-controlled leaderboard was archived in July 2026, and nothing measures the operator's own task on the operator's own data [runtime][nontech-startups].

**So what.** The durable products are the outer loop (evaluation, traces, regression gates) and an evaluation of real outcomes; a moat built on learning the harness has weak evidence behind it.

### 10. Generated interfaces are an output surface; the spreadsheet is the verification surface that already works

Raters preferred generated interactive pages to markdown 83% of the time, ignoring generation speed [academic][generated-ui]. Claude Code publishes live pages from a terminal session, and those pages now carry a shared database, presence and a page-side call to the model [generated-ui]. Nothing in the record shows a generated interface used to operate a long-running agent, and no HTML surface offers spreadsheet-grade provenance [generated-ui]. Meanwhile the office suite became agentic: Microsoft's Word, Excel and PowerPoint agents went GA in April 2026 and Anthropic's Excel, Word and PowerPoint add-ins in May 2026 [nontech-labs]. The grid is the one surface where a non-expert can check an agent's work cell by cell [generated-ui].

**So what.** Generate the view, fix the controls, and put the checking where checking already works: in the grid and the document, not on a new page.

### 11. Background execution is the real surface shift; interruption policy is thin and its evidence is anecdotal

One engine now runs from cloud schedules, repository events, HTTP triggers, messaging channels and a phone's push notification; the loop left the foreground [voice]. Cowork's scheduled tasks run with no device online; Codex automations wake themselves; ChatGPT has scheduled tasks [voice][nontech-labs]. Interruption controls are two push toggles, a presence file and a cap on idle check-ins; no vendor publishes an interruption-cost model [voice]. ChatGPT Pulse pushed unrequested output daily and was retired in July 2026, but neither vendor published why, it was Pro-only and mobile-only, and Google shipped the same idea as Daily Brief a month later [voice]. Voice is dictation in the most capable harnesses, and the confirmation problem, not latency, blocks it as a control surface [voice].

**So what.** The default harness runs unattended and reports back. Interruption policy is a real gap, but it is a judgment part of the kind vendors absorb, and the evidence for any particular policy is one withdrawn product with an unstated cause.

## Ten principles for a reimagined harness

1. **Split the harness, and do not own the inner half.** The inner harness is model-coupled and belongs to the model's owner; use the vendor's default, re-simplify on upgrades, keep a tested baseline. Build only in the outer layer. (Findings 1, 9)
2. **Verification is the product.** Every task should end with proof of work a non-expert can inspect: what changed, which claims rest on which sources, which checks ran, what could not be verified, and which actions of which consequence were taken. Documents and data need this more than code does, because they do not compile. (Findings 4, 10)
3. **Consequence classes are a vocabulary before they are a control.** Classify actions by reversibility, blast radius and cost so that proof and audit mean the same thing on every tool and surface; gate only where reversibility demands it and where a real enforcement point exists. (Findings 5, 6)
4. **The agent is a principal, not a proxy.** Its own identity, scoped and time-boxed grants, its own audit trail; never silently "as you". Expect the identity provider to own this. (Finding 5)
5. **Safe by structure, and safest by abstention.** Containment, credential-holding proxies, broken trifectas; a component that holds no credentials and never acts carries no blast radius. (Finding 6)
6. **Surfaces are projections, and the grid is the review surface.** Many, thin, wherever the person already is; check work in the spreadsheet and the document, generate views only for what the grid cannot show. (Findings 2, 3, 10)
7. **Design for attended, document-shaped work first.** Unattended multi-application runs are the exception a product must earn, because demand is thin and partnership is what workers say they want. (Finding 4)
8. **Cost is the first risk, not the ninth principle.** Reading is cheaper than generating; every added model call is a margin decision; price against a countable outcome. (Finding 7)
9. **Memory is data, not a moat.** Organization-owned, provenance-tagged, read-only by default, treated as an injection sink. (Finding 6)
10. **Own the evaluation of real outcomes**, because none exists, and measure the four numbers: approval load per task, delegation-regret rate, time to proof, cost per completed task. (Finding 9)

## What this means for the thesis

Supports: the harness is where the work and the incidents are, the surface is already separating from the engine, and the parts non-technical people need are missing everywhere. Contradicts: the inner harness is the model owner's, the default surface is settled and embedded, the labs hold the meter and rewrite the terms, and the neutral control plane is occupied by the company that owns identity and the office suite. Nuance: the thesis survives if restated as "the outer harness is the bottleneck; the part of it that an outsider can own is proof, not permission."

## Open questions and unverified claims

- The adoption split rests on vendor disclosures via secondary reports and on the Economic Index, which describes conversations, not people. Independent, controlled evidence exists only for developers, and METR declared its 2026 estimates unreliable [adoption].
- The 93%, 13.6%, 89% and 84% permission figures reach the corpus through secondary posts; the runtime document marks the approval rate as 93% or 97% depending on the report.
- Whether Anthropic's classifier targets OpenClaw's markers deliberately is unknown; the GitHub issues show behaviour only [strategy].
- Several 2026 figures were confirmed only through two or more independent search excerpts because primary pages were blocked; the verification notes in each document say which.
